Close the loop recurring-tasks opened: an occurrence knows it's due, but nothing tells the user. Making taskpilot reach out collides with its settled "only works when you run it" shape — that collision is the open question.

01The collision this feature must resolve

recurring-tasks D2 settled taskpilot as no daemon, no background jobs. A notification that arrives while you're not running tp requires… something running while you're not running tp. The draft deliberately does not pick a side.

done / skip / snooze — snooze never mutates the occurrence

02What is agreed (draft-level, N4–N6)

Dedup per occurrence — one "due", at most one "overdue" signal, however often checking runs. proposed

Quiet hours coalesce, never drop — a suppressed reminder resurfaces in the next allowed window. proposed

Everything off-switchable — globally and per template; scripts/non-TTY runs never ping. proposed

04Things to poke at

The reviewer's agenda for round 1. N1–N3 are fork-level: the PRD cannot settle — and the technical design cannot safely start — until they land.

N2 — Is a cron/systemd timer a violation of "no daemon" or a legitimate exception?
Everything hangs on this. Strictly-on-run keeps the product shape pure but means a user who doesn't run tp is never reminded — arguably making the feature a no-op for exactly the people who need it.
N1 — Who is this for: the terminal-dweller or the person who forgot the terminal exists?
Terminal summary reaches only users already running tp (story 6 vs story 1 tension). Desktop notifications reach everyone but add per-OS dependency weight to a dependency-free tool.
N3 — Is "overdue" a second notification or a state of the first?
Decides dedup semantics (N4) and whether a grace-period config exists at all.
Do we scope v1 to one machine explicitly?
A synced store file (Dropbox etc.) makes dedup state a cross-machine problem. Saying "one machine, v1" out loud is cheap now and expensive to retrofit later.
